# Bro

The last response did not land. Re-pitch it: same meaning, plainer words, nothing new.

The human calls you by name when a response went past him — too dense, too abbreviated, too far
inside your own head. The content was not the problem, so the content does not change.

Re-pitch only the immediately previous response. Open with the context it assumed — where the work
stands and why this came up — then make the point again.

Write the re-pitch in ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English:

- One idea per sentence. Short sentences, active voice, present tense.
- One word for one thing, and the same word every time that thing appears.
- Every abbreviation expanded at first use, and no shorthand you coined this session.
- Ordinary words in place of jargon, and no stacked nouns.

Take the names of things from the project's ubiquitous language: the project
[glossary](../../../dydo/glossary.md) for domain terms, and the locked
[dydo glossary](../../../dydo/reference/dydo-glossary.md) for the work-model terms. Describe
anything neither one names in plain words rather than coining a term for it.

Keep the technical meaning exact. Constraints, conclusions and uncertainty come through unchanged: a
qualifier you drop is meaning you deleted. Add no new analysis, no recommendation, no beginner
analogy, no tutorial — the re-pitch carries what was already said.

This is the corrective for agent-speak wherever it appears: any hat, any stage, any thread.

Stop after the re-pitch. The work continues where it left off.
